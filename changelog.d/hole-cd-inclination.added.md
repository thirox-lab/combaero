- **`EffusionPlateElement` and `FilmCoolingElement` take `cd_inclination="idelchik_3_2"`, an opt-in hole-angle term in the discharge coefficient.** By default the hole angle enters the Cd only through the drilled length `t/sin(angle)`, and those results are unchanged bit for bit. With the opt-in, the inlet coefficient of the `IdelchikThick` hole takes Idelchik's diagram 3-2 value for an inclined entrance in place of the normal sharp entrance's 0.5. The added loss is `0.3 cos(angle) + 0.2 cos^2(angle)`, which is 0.41 at 30 deg and 0 at 90 deg. Idelchik's Eq. (4-20) takes the inlet coefficient of a wall orifice from his diagrams 3-1 to 3-3, so the substitution follows the handbook. Keeping the thickness term tau at its normal-hole value, and applying the result to inclined cooling holes, is combaero's own composition. The term is reported as `Cd_inclination_dzeta`. `Cd_in_range` reads 0 below diagram 3-2's Re > 1e4 and below l/d 2, where the inlet jet has not yet reattached. Any other correlation refuses the opt-in. The GUI's effusion plate and film cooling nodes have a "Hole Angle Cd Term" selector, shown with `IdelchikThick`. The GUI hands the choice to the element unchanged, so a saved file that forces `"idelchik_3_2"` on another correlation is refused with a hint naming the field, not built without the term. Switching a node away from `IdelchikThick` resets a forced term to "auto".
- **New C++ `discharge_inclination_dzeta` and `DischargeHoleGeometry::inclination_deg`** (default 90, no term). The term composes with `Idelchik1966Thick` only, in the wall Cd and in the in-pipe loss. Every other discharge correlation throws for an inclined hole. The `(Cd, dCd/dRe, dCd/dU)` derivatives stay analytic.
- **`validation/cooling/hole_cd_inclination_runner.py` compares the term against Hay et al. (1983) and Gritsch et al. (1998), as cross-source accuracy.** It scores only the series with no coolant crossflow. On Gritsch's 30 deg hole the term moves the mean error from +6.4% to -4.3%. On Hay's 30 deg holes at Mc 0 and Minf ~0 it moves the error from +10% to -1% (parallel rig) and from +14% to +2% (perpendicular rig). Off, the predicted Cd(30)/Cd(90) at equal L/D is 1.00. On, it is 0.90, against 0.85-0.94 measured.
