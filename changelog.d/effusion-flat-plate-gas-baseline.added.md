- **`EffusionPlateElement(gas_baseline="flat_plate", gas_start_length=...)`: an opt-in flat-plate gas-side baseline.** By default the unblown coefficient that `gas_augmentation` multiplies is still the discharge chamber's own surface correlation, which for a smooth duct is fully developed, and those results are unchanged bit for bit. With `"flat_plate"` it is a turbulent boundary layer that restarts `gas_start_length` [m] upstream of the panel (at a bleed, a starter-film lip or a liner step), averaged over `panel_length`. The chamber still supplies the gas temperature and velocity. The flat-plate correlation is the unblown reference printed under every table of the Stanford full-coverage film-cooling data, NASA CR-2648's St_o, which is the reference published St/St_o augmentation ratios are measured against. New diagnostics report the x range and Re_x range used. `h_gas_unblown_extrapolated` flags a Re_x outside the data's 1.5e5-5e6; the value is still evaluated. The flat plate needs `panel_length` and a momentum-chamber discharge. The GUI's effusion plate node has a "Gas h Baseline" selector.
- **New C++ correlations `flat_plate_turbulent_nusselt_and_derivative` and `flat_plate_turbulent_panel_nusselt` (`_and_derivative`)**, with the `FlatPlatePanelNusselt` result. They give the local value and the closed-form panel average over [x0, x0 + L], each with its analytic derivative.
- **Behrendt et al. (2008) is now scored** by `validation/cooling/effusion_network_runner.py`. The runner builds the reacting 5-10 bar rig as a network and reads the plate's `eta_overall` on both gas baselines at `gas_augmentation` 1.0. These are cross-source accuracy results. The model over-predicts on all nine curves: by +21.6% on the chamber baseline and by +12.5% on a flat plate restarted at the rig's bleed. Both miss the coolant-temperature trend.
