- **Equilibrium combustion converges for H2 and CO fuel with a hydrocarbon (#1288).** H2 or CO fuel carrying a hydrocarbon, below about 500 K, often reported `converged=False`, and the equilibrium combustor's Jacobian raised. On main, 513 of 1000 drawn H2 states with a trace of methane (0.1-6.6 % air, 150-465 K) and 235 of 3000 drawn H2/CO states with methane to heptane did not converge. Now all of them do.
  - **Cause:** the element-potential start found the equilibrium, but it is written back to the Newton polish as reaction extents. A species that is no reaction's key then comes out as its amount plus the extents' changes. Far below those changes it cancels: CO2 at 2e-30 beside methane at 3e-5 (H2 fuel, 300 K) came back as -1e-21, and steam or H2 at 1e-22 to 1e-63 beside CO likewise. Which states failed depended on that rounding, so neighbouring states flipped.
  - **Now:** the joint solve takes a component basis where its scarcest non-key is more abundant than the current basis'. The most abundant independent species are the components (Smith and Missen), and every scarcer species keys its own formation from them.
  - **Jacobians:** where the old basis converged in this regime, its state carried that rounding. dT/dX was up to 13 % off a central difference (propane in CO fuel). It now matches to 1e-5.
  - **What moves elsewhere:** in a 6000-state draw over eleven fuels and blends (150-2000 K, 1e3-1e7 Pa) and a 4000-state fuel-only and trace-air draw, no state gains or loses convergence except 9 that now converge. States move by at most 3e-12 K.
